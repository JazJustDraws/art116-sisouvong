History

Prince William Forest Park is a diverse natural and cultural area located near Washington, D.C. Based in Northern Virginia, the park is 15,000 acres of secondary growth forest. Originally established as Chopawamsic Recreation Demonstration Area (RDA) in 1935, it soon became the model site that future RDA sites were patterned after. The Civilian Conservation Corp constructed five cabin camp units to support the RDA program. Today the park still operates the cabins as a way for visitors to enjoy the park.


Chopawamsic

Chopawamsic means 'by the small isolated lodge' in the language of the Algonquian people who once inhabited this land. In the Archaeic Period, Native Americans hunted and foraged, where they created tools out of wood, stone, and bone. Some of these artifacts can be found within multiple sites in the park.


Quantico Creek

Protection of a large portion of the Quantico Creek Watershed is an important natural function of the park. The North and South forks of Quantico Creek flow through the park. A large portion of the headwaters for the North fork of Quantico Creek is located in the park. Headwaters for the South fork of Quantico Creek are located on Marine Corps Base Quantico.


Partnerships between Prince William Forest Park and Marine Corps Base Quantico work to manage this vital resource. Both creeks can be enjoyed by walking along park hiking trails.

Preservation

An important part of preserving the historic integrity of Prince William Forest Park is preserving information about the park's varied past. The park has received six nominations to the National Register of Historic Places in part of its preservation of its four of five camp cabins.
